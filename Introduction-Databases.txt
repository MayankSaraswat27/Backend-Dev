
1.SQL databases are relational, structured into tables with fixed schemas, and use SQL for queries. They excel at complex joins and transactions (e.g., MySQL, PostgreSQL). NoSQL databases are non-relational, schema-flexible, and store data in formats like documents, key-value pairs, or graphs (e.g., MongoDB, Cassandra). They are better suited for handling large-scale, unstructured, or rapidly changing data.

2.The CAP theorem states that in a distributed system, you can only guarantee two of the following three: Consistency, Availability, and Partition Tolerance. Because network partitions are unavoidable, systems must choose between consistency (all nodes agree) and availability (every request gets a response). For example, MongoDB prioritizes availability and partition tolerance, sometimes sacrificing strict consistency.
